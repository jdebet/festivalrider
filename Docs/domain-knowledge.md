# Domain Knowledge

Definitions of concepts and time windows used in festival and concert scheduling.

## Venue open & close
Hours during which the venue is open. No event can be scheduled outside of these hours. It is the user's responsibility to ensure events fall within this window, or to adjust the opening hours in the schedule.

## Tech get-in
The hour at which the venue crew arrives. Currently indicative, but may in future be used to block or warn when scheduling get-ins, load-ins, or soundchecks before their arrival.

## Get-in
The time at which a band may arrive at the venue to announce their presence and/or meet the crew. They may drop off some items or visit their backstage, but no real gear is brought inside the venue yet.

## Load-in
The time at which a band may start loading their gear into the venue, a staging area, or directly onto the stage.

## Backstage drop
If a band cannot load in to the stage, this is the time to tour them around and guide them to their backstage, where they have free time before the soundcheck load-in.

## Setup
The time at which the band starts taking possession of the stage to set up their gear, and the venue crew begins assisting the band and providing what they need.

## Soundcheck
The time during which the band sets their sound, with the house crew or their own.

## Linecheck
A very short soundcheck where only microphones are checked for signal, or where the band runs through a single song and is done. Used when time is short but it is necessary to confirm everything works, not that it sounds great.

## Backstage lead
The time at which the band must leave their backstage and move to the stage's backstage, ready to change over with the other band.

## Changeover
The period during which one band removes their setup and the next band gets back on stage. Usually much quicker than setup during soundcheck because most equipment is out of cases, packed for speed, and ready to be plugged in. Occurs between two bands' shows.

## Doors
The time at which the audience may enter the venue. Usually signals that soundchecks must be finished by then. Typically 30 minutes to 1 hour before the first act or band starts.

## First show
The time at which the first show starts.

## Sound curfew
The time after which no band may still be playing, due to venue policy or legal restrictions. Bands may remain on stage to pack gear, or the merch stand may remain open to the public, but the show and music must stop.

## Backstage curfew
The time by which the band must have packed their gear on stage and left either the venue or be in the process of loading out. Their backstage closes at this time.

## Break
The time between the last soundcheck and the first show. This is the venue crew's break. All soundchecks must be finished, and nothing is usually scheduled during this window. Catering time often falls within the break, but the two are not linked.

## Gap
The buffer between the end of one soundcheck and the start of another (start of soundcheck here means the next band's load-in to stage, setup, or yes, soundcheck if everything is lumped into soundcheck). Usually provided as a time buffer and/or required by policy.
